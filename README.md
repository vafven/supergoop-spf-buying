## Start with the four formula families

Almost everything Supergoop makes is a variation on one of four bases. Once you know those, the 60-product catalogue collapses into a much shorter decision.

**Unseen Sunscreen SPF 50** is the flagship. It's a weightless, scentless gel that goes on completely invisible and doubles as a makeup primer — Supergoop's own product copy leans hard on the "100% invisible, no white cast" claim, and that's the whole reason it has a reputation. Fine for normal, dry, combination and oily skin. This is the safest default if you wear makeup or hate the feeling of sunscreen. Prices run from $19 for the small size up to $76 for the largest.

**Glowscreen SPF 40** is the tinted, dewy one. It has hyaluronic acid and niacinamide, and it's built to leave a radiant finish rather than a matte one. Good for dry or dull skin, bad if you're already shiny at 10am. Standard 1.7 oz size lists at $38, the value size at $48, and the mini at $19.

**PLAY Everyday Lotion SPF 50** is the workhorse. Fast-absorbing face-and-body lotion, water- and sweat-resistant for 80 minutes, and the one formula that's genuinely fine for kids and the beach. It runs $12 to $58 depending on size — the 18 oz bottle is the value play if you're coating a family.

**Mineral Unseen Sunscreen SPF 40** is the zinc-based alternative for people who can't do chemical filters. It's scentless and designed for sensitive skin, and Supergoop flags it as suitable for exactly that.

Everything else — the tinted primers, the body oil, the stick, the skin tint — sits on top of one of those four.

## Matching the product to your actual situation

Brand copy won't tell you this, but the choice usually comes down to four scenarios.

**You wear makeup most days.** Unseen SPF 50 or Protec(tint). Unseen sits under foundation without pilling; Protec(tint) replaces the foundation step entirely if you're happy with light coverage. The stick version ($32) is for touching up your face at lunch without wrecking what's already on.

**You're oily or acne-prone.** Unseen SPF 50 is formulated non-comedogenic and non-acnegenic, and it finishes natural rather than dewy. Mattescreen SPF 40 ($24) is the mattifying option if shine is the primary annoyance. Skip Glowscreen — radiant is the opposite of what you want.

**Your skin reacts to chemical filters.** Mineral Unseen SPF 40 or Mineral Mattescreen. Be realistic about the trade-off: mineral formulas are the ones most likely to leave a visible cast, and reviewers on Supergoop's own product page mention a slight white cast on Mineral Unseen even while praising the texture. On deeper skin tones, that matters more than the finish.

**You're outdoors, sweating, or buying for more than one person.** PLAY. It's the only part of the range designed for face and body, it holds up to 80 minutes of swimming or sweating, and the 18 oz size at $58 is the cheapest way to keep a household in sunscreen. The body mist ($18) exists for exactly one job: reapplying on your shoulders and legs at the beach without getting sand stuck to lotion.

One universal point: no formula here is a once-a-day product. Reapplication every two hours (or after 80 minutes of swimming or sweating) is what the directions say, and it's the reason a single 1.7 oz bottle doesn't last a summer.

## What's actually been tested, and what hasn't

Worth separating the marketing from the test data, because there's more of the latter than you'd expect for a suncare brand.

Unseen SPF 50's active ingredients are avobenzone 3%, homosalate 7%, octisalate 5% and octocrylene 9%. The brand cites an independent clinical study of 33 women over four weeks for the skin-clarity claim, and a separate study of 33 women measuring makeup wear eight hours after a single application. It's also listed as an Allure 2025 Best of Beauty winner, and it carries no oxybenzone.

PLAY Everyday Lotion is vegan, cruelty-free and gluten-free, and at Sephora it's flagged FSA/HSA eligible, which is genuinely useful if you have a flexible spending account you need to drain before it expires. Same 80-minute water and sweat resistance claim.

Mineral Unseen's own product page shows a 4.5 out of 5 rating across 231 reviews. Reading through them, the pattern is consistent: people like the matte, lightweight texture, and the recurring complaint is value — one reviewer's line is basically "good texture, I just wish you got more for $40." That's a fair summary of the entire brand. You're paying a premium for the texture and the finish, not for a novel UV filter.

## A buying checklist that saves money

1. Decide the formula first, size second. Buying the wrong finish to save $10 means buying twice.
2. Check the mini price against the full size. Minis are for testing, not for repurchasing.
3. Price the sets. If you'd use all the products in one, it's usually cheaper than two singles.
4. Look at whether the store you're buying from offers cashback before you check out — not after.
5. Buy the size that matches your usage. A 1.7 oz bottle used daily on face and neck does not last a season.

## Common questions

**Is Supergoop sunscreen worth the price?** For the invisible-gel finish, mostly yes — Unseen SPF 50 does something a lot of cheaper sunscreens don't, and it's the most-awarded product in the range. For everything else, it's a premium you can reasonably decide against, especially since the reviews flag value-for-size more than performance.

**What's the difference between Unseen and Mineral Unseen?** Filters. Unseen SPF 50 uses chemical UV filters in an invisible gel; Mineral Unseen SPF 40 uses mineral filters and is aimed at sensitive skin, with a higher chance of a slight white cast.

**Which Supergoop works for oily skin?** Unseen SPF 50 or Mineral Mattescreen SPF 40. Avoid Glowscreen unless you specifically want a radiant finish.

**Can I use it on my body?** PLAY Everyday Lotion and the PLAY body mist are designed for face and body. Using Unseen or Glowscreen on your whole body is an expensive habit.

**Does Supergoop sunscreen expire?** Like all sunscreen, it has a shelf life and loses effectiveness over time. If you've had a bottle through multiple summers, replace it.
